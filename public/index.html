Types of coverage

Match the protection to the need.

Most families end up with one of these four. We'll talk through which fits before we compare prices.

10, 20 or 30 years

Term life

Coverage for a set number of years, usually the lowest cost per dollar of death benefit. A common fit while you have a mortgage or kids at home.

Lifetime

Whole life

Permanent coverage with level premiums and a cash value that grows over time. Often used for estate planning and lifelong needs.

Flexible premiums

Universal life

Permanent coverage that lets you adjust premiums and the death benefit as life changes. Indexed versions tie cash value growth to a market index.

Smaller policies

Final expense

Whole life sized to cover funeral costs and final bills, so your family isn't left paying them. Some versions need no medical exam.

Common questions

Straight answers.

Can't find yours? Ask Nerses directly. A first conversation is free.

What does "independent agent" mean?

It means I'm not employed by a single insurance company. I'm contracted with more than 30 of them, so I can compare policies side by side and recommend the one that fits you, not the one I'm limited to selling.

Should I get term or whole life?

Term is built for a specific window, like the years you have a mortgage or kids at home, and costs less. Whole life lasts your entire life and builds cash value, at a higher premium. Many families use term for the big temporary need and add permanent coverage only if they have a lifelong reason for it.

Do I need a medical exam?

It depends on the company, your age and the amount of coverage. Some policies require an exam with blood and urine samples. Others use only health questions and database checks. Comparing companies helps find the simplest route for your situation.

I have life insurance through my job. Is that enough?

Group coverage from an employer is often a small multiple of your salary, and it usually ends when you leave the job. Many people keep it and add a personal policy that stays with them.

How soon can I be covered?

Timing varies by company and policy type. Policies without an exam can be approved faster than those that need medical underwriting. We'll talk about realistic timing when we compare your options.
